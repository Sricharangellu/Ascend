For a product as ambitious as ASCEND, the landing page should **feel like the operating system for an enterprise**, not like a POS, inventory app, or ERP from the 2000s.

When a CEO, COO, CFO, warehouse manager, buyer, or store manager logs in, they should immediately feel:

> **"This is the command center of my business."**

# Design Language

Aim for the feel of:

* Apple — clean and intuitive
* Linear — minimal and fast
* Notion — organized and flexible
* Stripe Dashboard — professional and data-rich
* Vercel Dashboard — modern and developer-friendly

Avoid the cluttered, dense interfaces common in legacy ERP systems like SAP ECC, Oracle E-Business Suite, or Microsoft Dynamics NAV.

## Core UX Principles

* **Role-based dashboards:** Executives, buyers, warehouse staff, finance teams, and store associates each see a homepage tailored to their responsibilities.
* **Progressive disclosure:** Show the most important information first, with deeper details one click away.
* **Action-oriented design:** Every insight should offer a direct next step.
* **Consistent visual hierarchy:** Use cards, whitespace, typography, and color to make scanning effortless.
* **AI-first experience:** Make the AI workspace a primary entry point, not an afterthought.

The goal is for users to open ASCEND and immediately understand the state of their business, identify what needs attention, and take action within seconds. That creates the feeling of an intelligent enterprise command center rather than just another ERP screen.
